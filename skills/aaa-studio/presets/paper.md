# Preset: paper (house style for any small game without a tuned preset). Art direction "Paper Craft": hand-cut card craft (name the game yourself; never call it "Paper Wall")
- Palette: a cream field sheet #faf3e5 on a torn kraft board #c4a57d, outer card #f4ead6. Ink #4a3426, muted ink #6b5242. Game pieces, no two neighbours alike: terracotta #bf6848, mustard #d9a94c, sage #97a37f, mint #a9b99e, dusty blue #7d97a5, coral #ee8468, plum #7f505b. Tough or special pieces cream #efe3ca, stitches #8a6a50; the player's piece washi gingham #98544a on #f6ecde.
- Shape language: no plain rectangles or circles. Every game piece is a cut-card tile: two card layers, a slightly larger, darker base card with a lighter inset card on top (the base shows about 3 px at the sides and top, 3.6 px below), corner radius 3.5–6 px and different per corner, edges wobbling ±0.4 px from a fixed per-piece seed, as if cut with scissors. Walls, cells, segments, blocks and pickups are all cut card on the cream sheet; leave a small even gap between neighbouring pieces. Tough or special pieces are cream cards stitched with a dashed thread 3 px inside the edge. The player's piece wears a gingham washi band. The field is a torn-edge sheet taped to the board. No slants.
- Readability: the pieces the player tracks must read at arm's length. The main mover (ball, head, falling piece) is at least 12 px with a 1 px ink-tinted rim and a contact shadow; the player's piece (paddle) is at least 64 x 12 px; the trail is clearly visible; nothing the player must see sits under 3:1 contrast with the sheet.
- Material and light: matte card, never shiny. Recipe, each tile drawn once to a sprite at device pixels: (1) one light from the top left (135°); (2) each layer gets two cached shadows only: contact 0.5/1 px offset, 1.1 px blur, 42% #4a2e1c, and ambient 1.2/2.3 px offset, 3.4 px blur, 20% (the top card casts 0.35/0.75 px, 0.9 px blur, 48% onto its base); (3) inside each top card's top-left edge a 0.5 px light crescent at 20% #fffaf0, inside its bottom-right edge a 0.5 px shade at 10% brown; (4) seeded fibre grain on every surface: specks of ±6% lightness and 0.4 px fibres at 6–10%; (5) torn edges: 1.2 px jagged noise every 1.3 px with a 1 px pale fibre rim #fffaf0; (6) sheet shading at most ±3%. No per-frame blur or filter.
- The feedback moments that make it an 8: (1) the game's main event (a hit, a pickup, a cleared line, a crash) tears or flutters paper: the piece tears along its own seeded jagged line into two halves that twist apart (up to 14°) and drift down, with about 13 ragged scraps with pale fibre edges and 26 small confetti bits, all gone by about 1.25 s; (2) the main mover leaves a short trail of 5 cream paper dots, shrinking and fading; (3) the player's piece gives a soft squash on contact (6% wider, 22% shorter, 180 ms) and a few paper dots hop off it. All bounded, seeded and short-lived.
- Motion: everything eased (cubic out). Reduced motion: nothing flies; a torn piece fades in place.
- HUD: pinned paper labels, not panels: a "LEVEL 1" tag, cut-paper title letters, the score on a kraft label, paper keycaps. Slab-serif system stack for words, rounded sans with tabular numbers; ink text at 8.3:1 or better.
- Sound: the main event is a paper tear: filtered seeded noise falling from about 2,400 to 900 Hz over 0.17 s, a short crinkle on top, and a soft wooden note that climbs with each event in a row. Contact with the player's piece is a soft card thump: a low sine dropping from about 170 to 112 Hz under a short dull paper tap. A low falling rustle when the game is lost.
- Avoid: gloss, glow, shine or see-through effects, dark backgrounds, plastic, clay or toy shine, heavy drop shadows, gradient panels, per-frame shadowBlur or filter, Math.random in look code, fat confetti, unbounded particles.
- Anchors: assets/anchors/brick/{dated,clean,current}.png, used as quality levels only (finish, material, feedback, cohesion); they show a brick-breaker, not this game.
- Picked for every game prompt without clear words for a tuned preset (brick or catch).
